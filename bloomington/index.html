How Bloomington Homeowners Can Extend HVAC Life

Most mid-season breakdowns start with maintenance that never happened, and the habits that prevent them are easy. Change your filter every one to three months, clear vegetation at least two feet from the outdoor condenser, and rinse the condenser coil every spring. Then book a professional tune-up twice a year, one for cooling and one for heating, so our technicians can catch small problems while they're still cheap to fix.

The age of your equipment matters just as much. With regular service, central air typically lasts 12 to 15 years, a furnace 15 to 20, and a heat pump 10 to 15. Once a system passes that range and starts acting up, like rising energy bills, rooms that won't reach the setpoint, or one repair after another, it's smart to start planning a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule is cheaper and is far less stressful than an emergency swap during the first heat wave of the year.

The sooner you call about a comfort problem, the smaller the repair tends to be. A weak capacitor is an easy, low-cost part to replace, yet that same capacitor, neglected for a season, can ruin the compressor and force a full system replacement.
